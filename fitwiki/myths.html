Fitness Myths Busted

Common gym myths that are simply not true.

MYTH

"Lifting makes women bulky"

TRUTH

Women have much lower testosterone than men. Lifting builds lean muscle, not bulk. Getting bulky requires years of work and often steroids.

MYTH

"Cardio kills muscle gains"

TRUTH

Moderate cardio 2-3 times a week is actually good for recovery. It only affects gains when you do too much without eating enough.

MYTH

"You must take protein right after workout"

TRUTH

The window is about 2 hours, not 30 minutes. Total daily protein intake matters far more than exact timing.

MYTH

"Do crunches to get a flat stomach"

TRUTH

You cannot spot reduce fat from one area. Fat loss happens everywhere through a calorie deficit. Abs show through diet, not crunches.

MYTH

"No pain no gain — always train to failure"

TRUTH

Training to failure every set increases injury risk and hurts recovery. Leave 1 to 3 reps in reserve on most sets.

MYTH

"Eating fat makes you fat"

TRUTH

Excess calories make you fat, not dietary fat. Healthy fats from nuts and eggs are important for hormones and brain function.

MYTH

"You need to train every single day"

TRUTH

Muscles grow during rest, not during workouts. Training breaks them down and rest rebuilds them. 3 to 5 training days per week is enough.

MYTH

"Supplements are necessary to build muscle"

TRUTH

You can build a great physique with zero supplements if your diet and training are consistent. Food always comes first.

MYTH

"Light weights with high reps tone you"

TRUTH

Toning means having muscle with low body fat. Both heavy and light weights build muscle. What matters is effort and diet.

MYTH

"Eating late at night causes weight gain"

TRUTH

Total calories determine weight gain, not when you eat. Eating at night is fine as long as you stay within your daily calorie limit.
