Injury and Recovery

Train smart and stay in the gym long-term.

Disclaimer: This page is for general information only. If you have an actual injury, see a doctor or physiotherapist before continuing to train.

Warm Up Properly

Never skip warming up. 5 minutes of light cardio and dynamic stretches before lifting reduces injury risk significantly.

Perfect Form First

Ego lifting is the number one cause of gym injuries. Master form before adding weight. If your form breaks down, the weight is too heavy.

Add Weight Slowly

Don't jump 10kg in one session. Add 2.5kg per week for upper body and 5kg for lower body. Slow progress is safe progress.

Work on Mobility

Poor hip, ankle, and shoulder mobility causes compensations that lead to injury. Stretch tight areas regularly, especially if you sit a lot during the day.

Listen to Your Body

Muscle soreness is normal. Sharp or joint pain is not. Stop any exercise that causes sharp or worsening pain during the set.

Take Rest Days

Overtraining causes injuries over time. Take at least 1 to 2 rest days per week. Every 6 to 8 weeks, take an easier lower volume week.

Sleep

Growth hormone is released during deep sleep. 7 to 9 hours of sleep is the single most important recovery tool available. No supplement replaces it.

Eat for Recovery

Have protein within 2 hours after training. Anti-inflammatory foods like turmeric, ginger, and fish oil can reduce soreness and speed healing.

Active Recovery

Light movement on rest days — a 20 minute walk, easy cycling, or yoga — increases blood flow and helps reduce next-day soreness.

Ice and Heat

Ice — first 48 hours after injury

Reduces swelling and pain. Wrap in cloth, do not apply directly to skin. 15 to 20 minutes at a time.

Heat — after 48 hours or for chronic tightness

Improves blood flow and relaxes tight muscles. Good for long-standing muscle tightness.

Foam Rolling

Slowly roll over tight muscles and pause on sore spots for 20 to 30 seconds. Good for quads, calves, and upper back. Best done after training or on rest days.

Hydration

Dehydration slows recovery. Drink 3 to 4 litres daily. More if you train hard or it is hot weather where you sweat more.

Shin Splints

Cause

Too much running too soon, hard surfaces, worn out shoes.

Symptoms

Dull ache along the inner shinbone during or after running.

Recovery

Rest from running. Ice for 15 minutes. Stretch calves. Wear proper shoes. Return to running gradually.

Rotator Cuff Strain

Cause

Overhead pressing with bad form, too much shoulder volume, muscle imbalances.

Symptoms

Pain when raising arm overhead or during pressing movements.

Recovery

Rest from overhead pressing. Light band external rotations to strengthen the cuff. See a physio if pain continues.

Knee Pain

Cause

Squatting with knees caving in, weak glutes, tight IT band.

Symptoms

Pain around or under the kneecap when squatting or going down stairs.

Recovery

Reduce squat depth temporarily. Strengthen glutes. Improve ankle mobility. Ice after training sessions.

Lower Back Pain

Cause

Rounding lower back on deadlifts or squats, weak core, sitting too long daily.

Symptoms

Dull ache or sharp pain in lower back after lifting. Morning stiffness.

Recovery

Rest from heavy lifting. Strengthen core with planks. Stretch hip flexors. Film yourself to check deadlift form.
