Nutrition Guide

Understanding what you eat is just as important as training.

The Three Macronutrients

🥩 Protein

Recommended: 1.6 to 2.2g per kg of bodyweight daily

Protein is the building block of muscle. When you train, your muscle fibers break down and protein is what repairs and rebuilds them. It also keeps you full longer than carbs or fat.

  • Chicken breast — 31g protein per 100g
  • Eggs — 13g protein per 100g
  • Paneer — 18g protein per 100g
  • Dal (cooked) — 9g protein per 100g
  • Greek Yogurt — 10g protein per 100g
  • Whey Protein Powder — 25g per scoop

🍚 Carbohydrates

Recommended: 45 to 65% of total daily calories

Carbs are your body's main fuel source, especially for training. They get stored as glycogen in muscles and give you energy to lift. Don't cut carbs when you are training hard.

  • Rice or Brown Rice
  • Oats
  • Sweet Potato
  • Whole Wheat Roti
  • Banana — great before workout
  • Fruits and Vegetables

🥑 Fats

Recommended: 20 to 35% of total daily calories

Fats are important for hormones, vitamin absorption, and brain function. Avoid trans fats from fried junk food. Focus on healthy fats from natural sources.

  • Almonds and Walnuts
  • Eggs (yolk)
  • Olive Oil or Coconut Oil
  • Peanut Butter
  • Fatty Fish like salmon or tuna

Other Basics

What is a Calorie?

A calorie is a unit of energy. Every food has calories. If you eat more than you burn, you gain weight. If you eat less, you lose weight. Total daily calories are the most important thing.

Meal Timing

Eat 3 to 5 meals a day. Try to have protein in every meal. Eat carbs around your workout. Total daily intake matters more than exact timing.

Hydration

Drink 3 to 4 litres of water every day. More if you sweat a lot. Dehydration reduces performance and slows recovery. Water is the best and cheapest supplement.

Pre and Post Workout

Eat carbs and protein 1 to 2 hours before training. Have protein within 2 hours after training to start muscle repair. A meal or protein shake both work.
